Notation, terminology and basic operations
Main points

A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers. Its size (or order) is written \( m \times n \): \( m \) rows, \( n \) columns — always rows first.

A matrix with 3 rows and 2 columns is \(3\times2\). A matrix with the same number of rows and columns (\( n\times n \)) is called a square matrix.

Element notation: \( a_{ij} \) means the entry in row \(i\), column \(j\) — row number always comes first, then column.

Addition / subtraction: only defined when both matrices are exactly the same size. Add or subtract corresponding entries.

Scalar multiplication: multiply every single entry by the scalar.

Examples / explanation

Size and elements:

\[ A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 5 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \] \(A\) is a \(2\times3\) matrix (2 rows, 3 columns).
\( a_{12} = 5 \) (row 1, column 2). \( a_{23} = 4 \) (row 2, column 3). \( a_{21} = 0 \).

Square matrix example:

\[ B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 6 \end{pmatrix} \] \(B\) is \(2\times2\) — square, since rows = columns.

Addition (same size only):

\[ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 1 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix} \] Add each pair of corresponding entries.

Subtraction:

\[ \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 1 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \]

Scalar multiplication:

\[ 3\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -6 \\ 0 & 12 \end{pmatrix} \] Every entry gets multiplied by 3, including the zero.
Helpful points
Addition/subtraction of matrices with different sizes is undefined — not zero, not an error you can force through, it simply doesn't exist. Always check sizes match first.
\( a_{ij} \): think "row, then column" — like reading coordinates on a grid, row first (like a street number), then column (like an avenue number).
A \(1\times n\) matrix is a row vector. An \(m\times1\) matrix is a column vector. Both are still "matrices" technically.
