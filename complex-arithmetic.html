What is a complex number, and how do we add, subtract, and multiply them?
Main points

The imaginary unit \(i\) is defined by \(i^2 = -1\).

A complex number has the form \(z = a + bi\), where \(a, b \in \mathbb{R}\).

  • \(a = \text{Re}(z)\) — the real part
  • \(b = \text{Im}(z)\) — the imaginary part (note: \(b\) itself is a real number)

Addition/subtraction: combine real parts and imaginary parts separately.

Multiplication: expand like binomials, then replace \(i^2\) with \(-1\).

The complex conjugate of \(z = a+bi\) is \(\bar{z} = a - bi\).

Examples / Explanation
\[(3+2i) + (1-5i) = 4 - 3i\] \[(3+2i) - (1-5i) = 2 + 7i\]
Multiply \((2+3i)(1-4i)\): \[= 2 - 8i + 3i - 12i^2\] \[= 2 - 5i - 12(-1) = 14 - 5i\]
Conjugate property: \(z\bar{z} = |z|^2 = a^2 + b^2\), always a real, non-negative number.
Divide using the conjugate: \[\frac{1+i}{2-i} = \frac{(1+i)(2+i)}{(2-i)(2+i)} = \frac{1+3i}{5} = \frac{1}{5} + \frac{3}{5}i\]
Helpful points
\(bi\) is called the "imaginary part" loosely — technically \(b\) alone is \(\text{Im}(z)\), and it's a real number.
To divide by a complex number, multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator.
Don't forget \(i^2 = -1\) when expanding — this is the single most common slip.
Powers of \(i\) cycle every 4: \(i^1=i,\ i^2=-1,\ i^3=-i,\ i^4=1\), then it repeats. To find \(i^n\), just look at \(n \bmod 4\).
