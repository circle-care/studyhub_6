How do we picture a complex number geometrically?
Main points

The Argand diagram (complex plane) plots \(z = a+bi\) as the point \((a,b)\).

  • Horizontal axis = real axis
  • Vertical axis = imaginary axis

Adding complex numbers follows the same parallelogram law as vector addition.

Conjugation \(z \to \bar{z}\) is a reflection in the real axis.

Negation \(z \to -z\) is a rotation of \(180°\) (reflection through the origin).

Examples / Explanation
Add \((1+2i)\) and \((3+i)\): plot both points, complete the parallelogram — the sum \(4+3i\) is the far corner, exactly like vector addition.
Helpful points
Don't confuse a point's position on the diagram with its modulus — the modulus is the *distance* from the origin, covered next page.
Reflecting a point in the real axis (flip the sign of \(b\)) always gives the conjugate.
