Raising to powers and finding roots
Main points

De Moivre's formula:

\[z^n = r^n(\cos n\theta + i\sin n\theta)\]

To find the \(n\) roots of \(z^n = w\) (with \(w = s(\cos\phi+i\sin\phi)\)):

\[z_k = s^{1/n}\left(\cos\frac{\phi+2\pi k}{n} + i\sin\frac{\phi+2\pi k}{n}\right)\]

for \(k = 0, 1, \dots, n-1\).

Roots of unity: the \(n\) solutions to \(z^n=1\) are equally spaced around the unit circle.

Examples / Explanation
Compute \((1+i)^4\): first convert to polar, \(1+i = \sqrt2\,e^{i\pi/4}\), then \[(1+i)^4 = (\sqrt2)^4 e^{i\cdot4\cdot\pi/4} = 4e^{i\pi} = -4\]
Solve \(z^3 = 1\): here \(s=1,\ \phi=0\), so \[z_k = \cos\frac{2\pi k}{3} + i\sin\frac{2\pi k}{3}, \quad k=0,1,2\] giving \(z_0 = 1\), \(z_1 = -\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\), \(z_2 = -\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\).
Helpful points
Always convert to polar/exponential form before applying De Moivre — it doesn't work directly on Cartesian form.
The \(n\) roots are always evenly spaced by an angle of \(\dfrac{2\pi}{n}\) around a circle of radius \(s^{1/n}\).
Don't forget: every non-zero complex number has exactly \(n\) distinct \(n\)th roots, not just one.
