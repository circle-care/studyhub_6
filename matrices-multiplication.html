Matrix multiplication (critical skill)
Main points
Compatibility rule: \(AB\) is only defined when the number of columns of \(A\) equals the number of rows of \(B\).

If \(A\) is \(m\times n\) and \(B\) is \(n\times p\), then \(AB\) is defined and has size \(m\times p\) — "outer numbers" give the result's size.

How to compute an entry: \((AB)_{ij}\) = (row \(i\) of \(A\)) \(\cdot\) (column \(j\) of \(B\)) — multiply corresponding entries and add them up.

Matrix multiplication is NOT commutative: \(AB \neq BA\) in general. Even if both products are defined, they can give completely different (or differently-sized) results.
Examples / explanation

Checking compatibility:

\(A\) is \(2\times3\), \(B\) is \(3\times2\). Columns of \(A\) (3) = rows of \(B\) (3) ✓ — so \(AB\) is defined, and has size \(2\times2\) (outer numbers: 2 and 2).

What about \(BA\)? Columns of \(B\) (2) = rows of \(A\) (2) ✓ — also defined, but gives size \(3\times3\). Same matrices, totally different shaped results!

Worked multiplication:

\[ A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 \end{pmatrix} \] \[ AB = \begin{pmatrix} (1)(5)+(2)(7) & (1)(6)+(2)(8) \\ (3)(5)+(4)(7) & (3)(6)+(4)(8) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 19 & 22 \\ 43 & 50 \end{pmatrix} \] Row 1 of \(A\) times column 1 of \(B\): \(1\times5 + 2\times7 = 19\). Row 1 times column 2: \(1\times6+2\times8=22\). Continue for row 2.

Showing \(AB \neq BA\):

\[ BA = \begin{pmatrix} (5)(1)+(6)(3) & (5)(2)+(6)(4) \\ (7)(1)+(8)(3) & (7)(2)+(8)(4) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 23 & 34 \\ 31 & 46 \end{pmatrix} \] Compare to \(AB = \begin{pmatrix}19&22\\43&50\end{pmatrix}\) — completely different matrix. Order matters.
Helpful points
Say the sizes out loud: "\(A\) is \(m\times n\), \(B\) is \(n\times p\)" — the middle two numbers must match, and the outer two numbers give you the answer's size.
Multiply row by column, never row by row or column by column. Each entry of the answer comes from one row of \(A\) and one column of \(B\).
Just because \(AB\) is defined does NOT mean \(BA\) is defined too — check both separately. And even when both are defined and the same size, they're usually still not equal.

\(2\times3\)\(3\times4\)Yes, → \(2\times4\)
