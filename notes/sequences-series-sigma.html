Evaluating series and sigma notation
Main points

A series is the sum of consecutive terms of a sequence.

Arithmetic series sum: \[ S_n = \frac{n}{2}\big(a + \ell\big) \] where \(n\) = number of terms, \(a\) = first term, \(\ell\) = last term.
Finite geometric series sum: \[ S_n = \frac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r} \]
Infinite geometric series: only sums to a finite value if \(|r|<1\): \[ S_\infty = \frac{a}{1-r} \]

Sigma notation: \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k\) means "sum the terms \(a_k\) starting at \(k=1\), ending at \(k=n\)."

Examples / explanation

Arithmetic series:

Sum of \(3+7+11+\cdots+79\) (first term 3, last term 79, common difference 4).

Find \(n\): \(79 = 3+(n-1)(4) \Rightarrow n=20\). \[ S_{20} = \frac{20}{2}(3+79) = 10(82) = 820 \]

Finite geometric series:

Sum of first 6 terms: \(a=2\), \(r=3\): \[ S_6 = \frac{2(1-3^6)}{1-3} = \frac{2(1-729)}{-2} = \frac{2(-728)}{-2} = 728 \]

Infinite geometric series (converges):

\(a=8\), \(r=0.5\) (\(|r|<1\), so it converges): \[ S_\infty = \frac{8}{1-0.5} = \frac{8}{0.5} = 16 \] Makes sense: \(8+4+2+1+0.5+\cdots\) keeps adding smaller and smaller amounts, approaching 16 forever without quite reaching past it.

Infinite geometric series (diverges — no sum exists):

\(a=5\), \(r=2\) (\(|r|>1\)): there is no finite sum — the terms keep growing, so the total grows without bound. \(S_\infty\) is undefined here.

Sigma notation, written and expanded:

\[ \sum_{k=1}^{5} 2k = 2(1)+2(2)+2(3)+2(4)+2(5) = 2+4+6+8+10 = 30 \]
Helpful points
Only use \(S_\infty = \dfrac{a}{1-r}\) when you've confirmed \(|r|<1\) first. Applying the formula blindly to \(|r|\geq1\) gives a meaningless number, not "no answer" — always check the condition before calculating.
For an arithmetic series, if you don't know the last term \(\ell\) but do know \(d\), find \(\ell\) first using \(\ell = a+(n-1)d\), then plug into \(S_n=\frac{n}{2}(a+\ell)\).
Reading \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\): the letter under \(\Sigma\) is the index variable (changes each term), the number below is where it starts, the number above is where it stops (inclusive).
