Continuity — quick reference
Always / never continuous
Always continuous (on their full domain):
  • Polynomials — \( x^2, x^3, 3x+1 \) etc.
  • \( \sin x \), \( \cos x \)
  • \( e^x \)
  • Constants — \( f(x) = c \)
  • \( |x| \)
NOT always continuous (breaks somewhere):
  • \( \tan x \) — breaks where \( \cos x = 0 \)
  • \( \dfrac{1}{x} \) — breaks at \( x = 0 \)
  • \( \ln x \) — undefined for \( x \le 0 \)
  • Piecewise functions — check the join points
  • \( \sqrt{x} \) — undefined for \( x < 0 \)
Fraction & composition rules

Fraction rule:

\( \dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)} \) is continuous everywhere except where \( g(x) = 0 \).

Always find and exclude the zeros of the denominator first.

Composition rule:

If \( f \) and \( g \) are both continuous, then \( f(g(x)) \) is continuous wherever \( g(x) \) is defined.

Continuous functions built from other continuous functions (by adding, multiplying, dividing off zeros, or composing) stay continuous.

Sums, products, differences:

If \( f(x) \) and \( g(x) \) are continuous at \( a \), then so are: \( f(x) + g(x) \), \( f(x) - g(x) \), \( f(x) \times g(x) \)
Decision tree

Is f(a) defined?

  • No → discontinuous (hole or undefined)
  • Yes → continue ↓

Does limx→a f(x) exist?

  • No (LH ≠ RH, or blows up, or oscillates) → discontinuous
  • Yes → continue ↓

Does the limit equal f(a)?

  • No → discontinuous (removable)
  • Yes → CONTINUOUS ✓
Fast check: if you can draw it at \( x = a \) without lifting your pen, it's continuous there.
