How far from the origin, and at what angle?
Main points

Modulus: \(|z| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) — the distance of \(z\) from the origin.

Argument: \(\theta\) — the angle measured from the positive real axis.

Every non-zero \(z\) has infinitely many arguments, differing by multiples of \(2\pi\).

The principal argument \(\text{Arg}(z)\) is the one in \((-\pi, \pi]\).

Properties: \(|zw| = |z||w|\), \(\left|\dfrac{z}{w}\right| = \dfrac{|z|}{|w|}\).

Examples / explanation
Find modulus and argument of \(z = 1 + i\sqrt{3}\): \[|z| = \sqrt{1^2 + (\sqrt3)^2} = \sqrt{4} = 2\] \[\theta = \arctan\!\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{1}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3}\] Point lies in the 1st quadrant, so \(\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}\) is correct as-is.
Find the argument of \(z = -1 - i\):
\(\arctan(1) = \frac{\pi}{4}\), but \(z\) is in the 3rd quadrant, so \[\text{Arg}(z) = \frac{\pi}{4} - \pi = -\frac{3\pi}{4}\]
Helpful points
\(\arctan(b/a)\) alone can give the wrong quadrant — always sketch the point first to check.
\(z\bar{z} = |z|^2\) — a fast way to compute the modulus without a square root, when convenient.
Argument is undefined for \(z = 0\) (no direction to measure).
