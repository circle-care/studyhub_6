What are limits?
Main points

A function \( f(x) \) describes what happens when input values change.

\( f(x) \) maps inputs (values of \( x \)) to outputs (values of \( f(x) \)).

Why limits matter:

📐 Derivatives — use limits to find the slope of a tangent to a curve

∫ Integrals — use limits to find area under a curve (rectangles getting thinner — Riemann sum)

One-sided limits

Right-hand limit — approaching from the right:

\[ \lim_{x \to 2^+} f(x) = 2 \]

Left-hand limit — approaching from the left:

\[ \lim_{x \to 2^-} f(x) = -2 \]

Tends to infinity

If outputs grow rapidly and do not slow down, the limit tends to \( \infty \).

Worked examples and information

Using the function:

\[ f(x) = \frac{\sqrt{3 - 5x} + 2x^2 + 23}{x - 1} \]

As \( x \to 1 \) (getting closer from the left):

Input \( x \)Output \( f(x) \)
0.5−1.87
0.9−1.93
0.99−1.997
0.999−1.9997
→ 1→ −2

So \( f(x) \) is getting closer to −2 as \( x \) approaches 1.

Checking if a limit tends to \( \infty \):

Show input values increasing by a factor of 10. If the output also increases by a factor of 10 each time and does not slow down, the limit tends to \( \infty \).

One-sided limits at x = 1

Helpful points
\( + \) superscript means approaching from the right
\( - \) superscript means approaching from the left

\( \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) \) = right-hand limit

\( \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) \) = left-hand limit

Important: A limit describes what the function is approaching — not necessarily what it equals at that point.
\( x \to a \) means \( x \) gets very close to \( a \) but does not have to equal \( a \).

If output keeps growing and does not slow down → tends to \( \infty \)
