Matrix powers, LaTeX notation, and equation-form nuances
Main points

Matrix powers: \(A^2 = A \times A\) — only defined if \(A\) is square. Uses full matrix multiplication, never entry-by-entry squaring.

LaTeX matrix environments:
pmatrix → \((\ )\) round brackets
bmatrix → \([\ ]\) square brackets
vmatrix → \(|\ |\) determinant bars

Equations → matrix form: every term has a coefficient, even invisible ones. \(x\) means \(1x\); \(-y\) means \(-1y\). Rows of \(A\) and entries of \(\mathbf{b}\) must stay matched to the same equation, in whatever order you choose.

Examples / explanation

Matrix power, worked:

\[ A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \] \[ A^2 = AA = \begin{pmatrix} (1)(1)+(2)(3) & (1)(2)+(2)(4) \\ (3)(1)+(4)(3) & (3)(2)+(4)(4) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & 10 \\ 15 & 22 \end{pmatrix} \]

LaTeX code for a matrix:

\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}

\[ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \] & separates columns, \\ starts a new row. Fractions inside a matrix use ordinary \frac{}{}, e.g. \frac{1}{2} in any cell.

Choosing equation order:

\[ \begin{aligned} x - y &= 1 \\ 2x + 3y &= 8 \end{aligned} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix} \] Listing \(x-y=1\) first instead of \(2x+3y=8\) is equally valid — just requires the \(\mathbf{b}\) vector to follow the same order (1, then 8).
Helpful points
Common mistake: \(A^2 \neq \begin{pmatrix}a_{11}^2 & a_{12}^2\\a_{21}^2&a_{22}^2\end{pmatrix}\). Squaring each entry individually is a different (wrong) operation — always do the full row-by-column multiplication.
Common LaTeX slip: \frac1{3} or \frac{1}3 — both wrong. \frac needs both arguments in their own braces: \frac{1}{3}.
A matrix must be square before you can even ask about \(A^2\), \(A^3\), or \(A^{-1}\) — non-square matrices don't have powers or inverses, full stop.
