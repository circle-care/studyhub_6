What do the limit laws tell us about continuity?
Main points

If \( f \) and \( g \) are both continuous at \( a \), the limit laws let us build lots of new continuous functions out of them.

Continuous under:
Addition, subtraction, multiplication — always.
Division — as long as the denominator isn't zero.
Composition — always.
Any polynomial is continuous on the entire real line — it's built entirely from constants and \( x \) using just sums and products.
Examples / explanation

① Sum, difference, product: if \( f \) and \( g \) are continuous at \( a \), so are \( f+g \), \( f-g \), \( f \times g \).

\( \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a) \) and \( \lim_{x \to a} g(x) = g(a) \)

\[ \lim_{x \to a} [f(x)g(x)] = f(a)g(a) \] So \( h(x) = f(x)g(x) \) is continuous at \( a \).

② Quotient: if \( g(a) \neq 0 \), then \( f/g \) is continuous at \( a \).

\( \tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} \) — continuous everywhere except where \( \cos x = 0 \), i.e. \( x = \pm\frac{\pi}{2}, \pm\frac{3\pi}{2}, \dots \)

③ Composition: if \( g \) is continuous at \( a \) and \( f \) is continuous at \( g(a) \), then \( f \circ g \) is continuous at \( a \).

\( \sin(x^2+1) \) — inner function \( x^2+1 \) is continuous everywhere, outer function \( \sin \) is continuous everywhere, so the composition is continuous everywhere.
Helpful points
Division law only holds where \( g(a) \neq 0 \). Where the denominator hits zero, continuity breaks — check for those points first.
\( \dfrac{1}{x^2+1} \) is a polynomial over a polynomial, and the denominator is never zero — so this is continuous everywhere, no exceptions.

These five rules — sum, difference, product, quotient, composition — cover almost every function you'll build in this course.

Extra / summary
OperationContinuous when...
\( f + g \), \( f - g \)always, if \( f \), \( g \) continuous
\( f \times g \)always, if \( f \), \( g \) continuous
\( f / g \)always, provided \( g(a) \neq 0 \)
\( f \circ g \)always, if \( g \) continuous at \( a \), \( f \) continuous at \( g(a) \)
