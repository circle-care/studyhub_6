✏️ Exam Prep — Complex Numbers

Quick revision, then 10 practice questions from foundation to advanced

Quick revision

1. Arithmetic & the imaginary unit

\(i^2=-1\). Write \(z=a+bi\). Add/subtract by combining real and imaginary parts separately. Multiply like brackets, replacing \(i^2\) with \(-1\).

Conjugate: \(\bar z = a-bi\), and \(z\bar z=a^2+b^2\). Divide by multiplying top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator.

2. The Argand diagram

Plot \(z=a+bi\) as the point \((a,b)\). Addition follows the parallelogram rule, same as vectors.

Conjugation = reflection in the real axis. Negation = 180° rotation about the origin.

3. Modulus & argument

\(|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) (distance from origin). Argument \(\theta\) is the angle from the positive real axis.

Always sketch the point first — a raw \(\arctan(b/a)\) ignores the quadrant. Principal argument lies in \((-\pi,\pi]\).

4. Polar & exponential form

\(z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)=re^{i\theta}\). Multiplying: add the angles, multiply the moduli. Dividing: subtract the angles, divide the moduli.

Euler: \(e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\).

5. De Moivre's formula & roots

\(z^n=r^n(\cos n\theta+i\sin n\theta)\). For roots of \(z^n=w\), add \(2\pi k\) before dividing the angle by \(n\), for \(k=0,\ldots,n-1\).

Convert to polar/exponential form first — De Moivre never applies directly to Cartesian form.

6. Polynomials & the FTA

Every degree-\(n\) polynomial has \(n\) complex roots (Fundamental Theorem of Algebra). With real coefficients, complex roots always come in conjugate pairs.

Negative discriminant \(\Rightarrow\) conjugate pair of roots for a quadratic.
Practice questions — foundation to advanced
1
Foundation
Simplify \((4-3i)+(2+5i)\).
Show worked answer
Combine real and imaginary parts separately
\[(4+2)+(-3+5)i\]
Answer: \(6+2i\)
2
Foundation
Multiply \((3+2i)(1-4i)\).
Show worked answer
Expand like brackets
\[3-12i+2i-8i^2\]
Substitute \(i^2=-1\)
\[3-10i-8(-1) = 3-10i+8\]
Answer: \(11-10i\)
3
Core
Simplify \(\dfrac{2+i}{3-i}\), giving your answer in the form \(a+bi\).
Show worked answer
Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator
\[\frac{(2+i)(3+i)}{(3-i)(3+i)}\]
Expand top
\[6+2i+3i+i^2 = 6+5i-1 = 5+5i\]
Expand bottom
\[3^2+1^2=10\]
Answer: \(\dfrac{5+5i}{10} = \dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}i\)
4
Core
Find the modulus and principal argument of \(z=-4+4i\).
Show worked answer
Modulus
\[|z|=\sqrt{(-4)^2+4^2}=\sqrt{32}=4\sqrt2\]
Quadrant check
Real part negative, imaginary part positive → 2nd quadrant.
Reference angle then adjust
\[\arctan\!\left(\frac{4}{4}\right)=\frac{\pi}{4} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \theta = \pi-\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac{3\pi}{4}\]
Answer: \(|z|=4\sqrt2\), \(\text{Arg}(z)=\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\)
5
Core
Write \(z=-3-3\sqrt3\,i\) in exponential form.
Show worked answer
Modulus
\[r=\sqrt{(-3)^2+(-3\sqrt3)^2}=\sqrt{9+27}=\sqrt{36}=6\]
Quadrant check
Both parts negative → 3rd quadrant.
Reference angle then adjust
\[\arctan\!\left(\frac{3\sqrt3}{3}\right)=\frac{\pi}{3} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \theta = \frac{\pi}{3}-\pi=-\frac{2\pi}{3}\]
Answer: \(z=6e^{-i2\pi/3}\)
6
Challenging
Given \(z=4e^{i\pi/6}\) and \(w=2e^{i\pi/2}\), find \(zw\) and \(\dfrac{z}{w}\), each in exponential form with the argument in \((-\pi,\pi]\).
Show worked answer
Multiplication — moduli multiply, arguments add
\[zw = (4)(2)\,e^{i(\pi/6+\pi/2)} = 8e^{i2\pi/3}\]
Division — moduli divide, arguments subtract
\[\frac{z}{w} = \frac{4}{2}\,e^{i(\pi/6-\pi/2)} = 2e^{-i\pi/3}\]
Answer: \(zw=8e^{i2\pi/3}\), \(\dfrac{z}{w}=2e^{-i\pi/3}\) (both already in range)
7
Challenging
Use De Moivre's formula to find \((1-i)^6\), giving your answer in Cartesian form.
Show worked answer
Convert to exponential form
\[|1-i|=\sqrt2, \qquad \theta=-\frac{\pi}{4} \text{ (4th quadrant)}\] \[1-i = \sqrt2\,e^{-i\pi/4}\]
Apply De Moivre's formula
\[(1-i)^6 = (\sqrt2)^6\,e^{-i6\pi/4} = 8e^{-i3\pi/2}\]
Bring the angle into range
\[-\frac{3\pi}{2}+2\pi = \frac{\pi}{2} \quad \text{(now inside range)}\]
Convert back to Cartesian
\[8e^{i\pi/2} = 8(\cos\tfrac{\pi}{2}+i\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2}) = 8(0+i) \]
Answer: \(8i\)
8
Challenging
Find all solutions to \(z^3=-8\), giving your answers in Cartesian form.
Show worked answer
Write −8 in polar form
\[-8 = 8(\cos\pi+i\sin\pi)\]
Apply the roots formula, n = 3
\[z_k = 8^{1/3}\left(\cos\frac{\pi+2\pi k}{3}+i\sin\frac{\pi+2\pi k}{3}\right),\quad k=0,1,2\] \[8^{1/3}=2\]
Work out each angle
\(k=0:\ \frac{\pi}{3}\)   \(k=1:\ \pi\)   \(k=2:\ \frac{5\pi}{3}\to-\frac{\pi}{3}\) (adjusted into range)
Convert each to Cartesian
\[z_0 = 2\left(\cos\tfrac{\pi}{3}+i\sin\tfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = 1+\sqrt3\,i\] \[z_1 = 2(\cos\pi+i\sin\pi) = -2\] \[z_2 = 2\left(\cos(-\tfrac{\pi}{3})+i\sin(-\tfrac{\pi}{3})\right) = 1-\sqrt3\,i\]
Answer: \(z=-2,\ \ 1+\sqrt3\,i,\ \ 1-\sqrt3\,i\)
9
Advanced
Given that \(z=2-i\) is a root of \(z^3-6z^2+13z-10=0\), find all three roots.
Show worked answer
Apply the Conjugate Root Theorem
Coefficients are real, so \(2+i\) must also be a root.
Build the quadratic factor from this conjugate pair
\[(z-(2-i))(z-(2+i)) = z^2-4z+5\]
Divide the cubic by this quadratic to find the remaining factor
\[z^3-6z^2+13z-10 = (z^2-4z+5)(z-2)\]
Answer: \(z=2,\ \ 2-i,\ \ 2+i\)
10
Advanced
Find all solutions to \(z^4 = -8+8\sqrt3\,i\), giving your answers in exponential form with the argument in \((-\pi,\pi]\).
Show worked answer
Convert the right-hand side to polar form
\[r=\sqrt{(-8)^2+(8\sqrt3)^2}=\sqrt{64+192}=\sqrt{256}=16\] 2nd quadrant (real negative, imaginary positive): \[\arctan\!\left(\frac{8\sqrt3}{8}\right)=\frac{\pi}{3} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \theta=\pi-\frac{\pi}{3}=\frac{2\pi}{3}\]
Apply the roots formula, n = 4
\[z_k = 16^{1/4}\,e^{i\left(\frac{2\pi/3+2\pi k}{4}\right)}, \quad k=0,1,2,3, \qquad 16^{1/4}=2\]
Work out each angle (adding a quarter turn, π/2, each time)
\(k=0:\ \dfrac{\pi}{6}\)   \(k=1:\ \dfrac{\pi}{6}+\dfrac{\pi}{2}=\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\)   \(k=2:\ \dfrac{2\pi}{3}+\dfrac{\pi}{2}=\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\to-\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\)   \(k=3:\ -\dfrac{5\pi}{6}+\dfrac{\pi}{2}=-\dfrac{\pi}{3}\)
Answer: \(z = 2e^{i\pi/6},\ \ 2e^{i2\pi/3},\ \ 2e^{-i5\pi/6},\ \ 2e^{-i\pi/3}\)
