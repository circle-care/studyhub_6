✏️ Exam Prep — Sequences & Series

Quick revision, then 10 practice questions from foundation to advanced

Quick revision

1. Notation & types

Three ways to specify a sequence: listing, closed form, recurrence. Arithmetic: \(x_n=a+(n-1)d\). Geometric: \(x_n=ar^{n-1}\).

Check consecutive differences (arithmetic) or ratios (geometric) — verify with more than one pair.

2. Convergence

Sequences converge (settle to a limit), diverge (grow unboundedly), or oscillate (never settle).

For geometric: \(|r|<1\) converges to 0, \(r>1\) diverges, \(r\leq-1\) oscillates.

3. Series & sigma notation

A series is the sum of a sequence's terms. \(\sum_{k=1}^n a_k\) means add \(a_k\) from \(k=1\) to \(n\).

Arithmetic sum: \(S_n=\frac{n}{2}(2a+(n-1)d)\). Geometric sum: \(S_n=\frac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}\).

4. The binomial theorem

\((a+b)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom{n}{k}a^{n-k}b^k\). Powers of \(a\) decrease, powers of \(b\) increase, always summing to \(n\).

Watch signs carefully when \(b\) is negative — odd powers keep the negative sign.

5. Standard series

\(\sum k = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}\), \(\sum k^2=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}\), \(\sum k^3=\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)^2\).

Sums not starting at \(k=1\): split as a difference of two sums that do.

6. Tricky examples

Recurring decimals are geometric series in disguise. Use \(S_\infty=\frac{a}{1-r}\), or the algebraic ×10/×100 subtraction method.

Always simplify the final fraction to lowest terms.
Practice questions — foundation to advanced
1
Foundation
Find \(x_8\) for the arithmetic sequence with \(a=7\), \(d=4\).
Show worked answer
Apply x_n = a + (n-1)d
\[x_8 = 7+(7)(4) = 7+28\]
Answer: 35
2
Foundation
Find \(x_5\) for the geometric sequence with \(a=2\), \(r=3\).
Show worked answer
Apply x_n = ar^(n-1)
\[x_5 = 2(3)^4 = 2(81)\]
Answer: 162
3
Core
Does the sequence \(a_n=\dfrac{2n+3}{n}\) converge, diverge, or oscillate? If it converges, find the limit.
Show worked answer
Rewrite as a sum of simpler terms
\[a_n = 2+\frac{3}{n}\]
Consider what happens as n grows
As \(n\to\infty\), \(\frac3n\to0\), so \(a_n\to2\).
Answer: converges to 2
4
Core
Evaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{6}k\) using the standard formula.
Show worked answer
Apply the formula n(n+1)/2
\[\frac{6(7)}{2}\]
Answer: 21
5
Core
Expand \((x-3)^3\) using the binomial theorem.
Show worked answer
Use coefficients 1, 3, 3, 1 with a = x, b = -3
\[x^3 + 3x^2(-3) + 3x(-3)^2 + (-3)^3\] \[= x^3-9x^2+27x-27\]
Answer: \(x^3-9x^2+27x-27\)
6
Challenging
Find the sum to infinity of the geometric series with \(a=8\), \(r=0.5\).
Show worked answer
Check |r| < 1 (required for convergence)
\(|0.5|<1\), so the sum to infinity exists.
Apply S∞ = a/(1-r)
\[S_\infty = \frac{8}{1-0.5} = \frac{8}{0.5}\]
Answer: 16
7
Challenging
Evaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{5}(4k^2-3k)\).
Show worked answer
Split using the sum/difference rule, pull out constants
\[4\sum_{k=1}^5 k^2 - 3\sum_{k=1}^5 k\]
Apply the standard formulas (n = 5)
\[4\left(\frac{5(6)(11)}{6}\right) - 3\left(\frac{5(6)}{2}\right) = 4(55)-3(15)\] \[= 220-45\]
Answer: 175
8
Challenging
Find the coefficient of \(x^3\) in the expansion of \((x+2)^5\).
Show worked answer
Identify which term gives x³ (power of a is n-k)
Need \(n-k=3\) with \(n=5\), so \(k=2\).
Apply the binomial coefficient formula
\[\binom{5}{2}x^3(2)^2 = 10\times x^3\times4 = 40x^3\]
Answer: 40
9
Advanced
Convert \(0.\overline{27}\) (i.e. \(0.272727\ldots\)) to a fraction in lowest terms.
Show worked answer
Set up x and multiply by 100 (two digits repeat)
\[x = 0.272727\ldots \qquad 100x = 27.272727\ldots\]
Subtract to cancel the repeating part
\[100x-x = 27 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 99x=27\]
Solve and simplify
\[x = \frac{27}{99} = \frac{3}{11}\]
Answer: \(\dfrac{3}{11}\)
10
Advanced
Evaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=6}^{10}k^2\) by splitting into two sums that start at \(k=1\).
Show worked answer
Split as a difference of two standard sums
\[\sum_{k=6}^{10}k^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{10}k^2 - \sum_{k=1}^{5}k^2\]
Apply the standard formula to each
\[\frac{10(11)(21)}{6} - \frac{5(6)(11)}{6} = 385-55\]
Answer: 330
