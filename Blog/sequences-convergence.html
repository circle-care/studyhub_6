Part 2 of 6: Convergence

Sequences · Part 2

Where does a sequence end up?

Convergence, divergence, and oscillation — what happens to a sequence as n gets large.

📝 View the quick-reference notes sheet for this topic →

Three possible behaviours

As \(n\) gets larger and larger, a sequence's terms can behave in one of three ways:

Converges: the terms settle down and get arbitrarily close to a single fixed number, called the limit.

Diverges: the terms grow without bound (towards \(+\infty\) or \(-\infty\)), never settling anywhere.

Oscillates: the terms keep jumping between values (or ranges of values) without ever settling down.

Examples of each behaviour

Converges
\(a_n = \dfrac{1}{n}\): the terms are \(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac13, \tfrac14,\ldots\), getting closer and closer to 0 — this sequence converges to 0.
Diverges
\(a_n = n^2\): the terms are \(1,4,9,16,\ldots\), growing without any bound — this sequence diverges to \(+\infty\).
Oscillates
\(a_n = (-1)^n\): the terms are \(-1,1,-1,1,\ldots\), forever alternating — this sequence oscillates and has no limit at all.

Geometric sequences and the value of r

For a geometric sequence \(x_n=ar^{n-1}\), the value of the common ratio \(r\) completely determines its long-term behaviour:

\(|r|<1\): converges to 0 (each term shrinks towards zero)
\(r=1\): constant sequence, converges (trivially) to \(a\) itself
\(r>1\): diverges to \(+\infty\)
\(r\leq-1\): oscillates (alternates sign, and if \(|r|>1\), also grows in size without bound)
Worked example
For \(x_n = 5(0.8)^{n-1}\): since \(|0.8|<1\), this sequence converges to 0 — each term is 0.8 times the one before, shrinking steadily.

Try it yourself

Try it: Does \(a_n = \dfrac{3n+1}{n}\) converge, diverge, or oscillate? (Hint: rewrite it as \(3+\dfrac{1}{n}\) first.)
Show answer
Rewriting: \(a_n = 3+\dfrac1n\). As \(n\) grows, \(\dfrac1n\to0\), so \(a_n\to3\). This sequence converges to 3.
Try it: A geometric sequence has \(r=-2\). Does it converge, diverge, or oscillate?
Show answer
Since \(r=-2\leq-1\), this sequence oscillates — it alternates in sign every term, and since \(|r|=2>1\), the size of the terms also grows without bound each time.
